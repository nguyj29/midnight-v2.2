# Track p06

**What it is.** A 3:12 melancholic, guitar-led instrumental in B minor (the chroma guess, D major, is the relative major) at a steady 100 BPM, 4/4. A clean guitar (the loudest stem) loops a figure, high piano fills decorate it, and a tight programmed beat keeps time. There is no bass stem (−72 dBFS), so the guitar's low strings carry the bass. It sits between lo-fi/chill beat music and mellow indie/folk guitar.

**How it develops.**

**What's striking.** The G–F# rub over the falling bass tints each chord maj7/maj9 (19 of each among the note-fitted chords), which is simple but wistful. Drummed and drumless stretches alternate, and the closing ritardando with its open ending on the fifth fades out instead of stopping hard.

**What's weak or questionable.** It's very repetitive: one 4-bar progression runs nearly throughout, and 2:05–2:43 copies 1:07–1:45. There's no distinct lead melody, only the riff's F#–G–F# neighbour-note wiggle. Dense mastering (−10.5 LUFS, LRA 2.5 LU) means the breakdowns lose drums but not loudness, which flattens the contrast. With no bass, the low end rests on the kick.

These fit a plucked guitar lead with piano, and I favour guitar fairly confidently. "Harp" likely reflects the plucked arpeggios. Bossa probably responds to the maj7 colour, but the straight programmed beat doesn't support a bossa reading. The mood tags match mine.

**In plain words.** A soft, wistful piece built on one guitar pattern that keeps sinking downward like a sigh, over a steady, head-nodding beat. Twinkly piano drifts in and out, and now and then the beat falls away for a quiet, floating moment. It feels nostalgic and a little sad, like music for a rainy window or a late-night drive. At the end it slows and just hangs in the air. It's calming, but it barely changes, so anyone waiting for a real tune or a lift may drift off.
