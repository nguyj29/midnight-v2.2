# Taste profile — version 2 (revised once after round 1 validation)

Built from the user's order of 18 tracks (`../labels.csv`) and the listening notes. The user left no comments, so
everything here is read off the music. Track references use the user's position (S1 = their favourite, B4 = their
least favourite) plus a short description.

**How sure is each pattern?** **Strong** = several tracks on each side agree and nothing clearly contradicts it.
**Moderate** = a clear trend with an exception or two. **Hunch** = rests on one or two tracks.

## The short version

The favourites are **atmospheric and immersive**: music that sets a mood (cool, dark, night-time, melancholy or
hypnotic) and pulls you into a sound world, often with a beat you can nod to. They also tend to **breathe**:
things drop away and come back. The least favourite tracks are either **sweet and pastel** (sunny, cute,
gentle-pretty) or **tense and theatrical** (urgent "trailer" drama, sinister and relentless). The middle tier (A)
is mostly energetic or loop-based tracks that are fun but stay at one level: action-game rushes, club loops and
hip-hop beats.

## What changed after validation round 1
Round 1 (`validation/round1_metrics.md`) placed three S tracks too low (S1 trance, S3 heavy guitars, S7 trip-hop)
and one B track too high (B2 dark rock riff). Three changes, marked **[v2]** below:
1. **Impact counts.** The user's top three all have a big, physical *arrival*: the drop landing, the band
   crashing in, a tight groove kicking in hard. Version 1 treated them as merely "energetic". New pattern 1b.
2. **"Dark" splits in two.** Melancholy, cool or hypnotic darkness is liked. Sinister, uneasy, menacing darkness
   is not; it counts with "tense". Pattern 1 and pattern 7 are updated.
3. **Game or anime flavour is not a penalty in itself.** It was standing in for "cute" or "melodramatic" moods,
   which pattern 1 already covers. The penalty is dropped (pattern 5 is kept as a description only).

## Patterns

### 1. Mood: immersive and cool beats sweet or tense — **strong**
- S tier: the cool, moody house groove (S2), the heavy, sad wall of guitars (S3), the dark, restless breakbeat
  piece (S4), the calm, bittersweet string swell (S5), the warm, hypnotic downtempo track (S6) and the sparse
  2 a.m. trip-hop (S7). Six of seven are dark, melancholy or dreamy-hypnotic.
- B tier: the urgent movie-trailer strings (B1), the sinister, pounding rock riff (B2), the pastel, nostalgic
  game-keys piece (B3) and the sunny new-age "focus" loop (B4). All four are either **tense and theatrical** or
  **sweet and pastel**.
- The one S track that is happy and bright (S1, an upbeat trance/EDM track) is *driving* dance music, not cute.
  Brightness alone isn't the problem. Gentle, pretty-sunny is.
- **[v2]** Dark isn't enough on its own. Sad, cool or hypnotic dark (S3, S4, S7) is liked, but sinister, uneasy dark
  (B2's creeping riff) goes with the tense group.

### 1b. [v2] Impact: a big, satisfying arrival — **moderate**
The top three are the most physically hard-hitting pieces in the set: S1's drop after a muffled, filtered intro,
S2's tight broken beat landing after a filter sweep, and S3's band crashing in after a quiet, clean intro. S4's
drums-only break slamming back into the full band is the same move. Tracks with no real arrival sit lower: B4
(no drums, no drop), B1 (layers pile up without a landing) and A6 (starts and stays). An arrival helps most
when the mood is also right: A5's heroic climax lands, but in a melodramatic mood, so it stays in A.

### 2. The arrangement breathes: drops, breaks, swells — **moderate**
- Most S tracks have a strong contrast moment: the drums-only break in S4, the drumless floating breaks in S6, the
  near-silent drop in S7, the clean intro crashing into the wall in S3, the filtered intro and drop in S1 and S2,
  and the slow swell in S5.
- Several A and B tracks sit at one level from start to finish: A1 (fast action rush), A2 (club house loop for
  2.5 minutes), A3 and A6 (hip-hop loops), B1 (trailer strings) and B2 (rock riff).
- Numbers: average loudness range (how much the level moves) is 7.3 in S, 5.4 in A and 4.3 in B. **Confound
  check:** loudness range is partly a mastering choice, but here it tracks real arrangement moves (drumless
  breaks, drum-only breaks, quiet intros), not squashing, and on its own it barely predicts the order
  (ρ = +0.24, p = 0.34).
- Exceptions: A5 (music-box to cinematic climax) and A7 (live jazz-band groove) both breathe a lot and still sit
  in A. B3 and B4 aren't flat, but they are the least liked.

### 3. Sweet, "pretty" chord colour isn't rewarded — **moderate**
The tracks with the most lush, pastel chord colour (the dreamy, soft-focus sound) sit low. Examples are B3 (a
floating two-chord keys loop) and B4 (a rippling, sunny arpeggio). The measured "chord richness" correlates
*negatively* with the order (ρ = −0.50, p = 0.03). S6 is lush too, but it sits on a nodding beat with a darker,
wistful pull. The user seems to like colour when it's moody, not when it's sweet.

### 4. Cinematic music: calm beats heroic beats urgent — **moderate (three tracks)**
Calm, immersive cinematic music ranks highest (S5, drumless string swell). A build to a heroic climax (A5) is
in the middle. Urgent, driving trailer music (B1) is near the bottom. Same broad style, three tiers, ordered by
how *tense and pushy* it is.

### 5. Game and anime-flavoured music lands in A or B — **description only [v2]**
Five of six tracks that sound like video-game or anime music sit outside S: A1 (anime action), A4 (loud game
synth loop), A5 (anime-cinematic build), B1 (game/trailer cue) and B3 (nostalgic game keys). The exception, S1,
is a polished club track first and game-like second. **[v2]** This isn't used as a penalty any more: these
tracks sit low because of their mood (cute, heroic or tense) and their one-level energy, not their genre.

### 6. Hip-hop loop beats sit in the middle unless they're very dark and sparse — **moderate (three tracks)**
The slow nodding beat with a key-change detour (A3) and the dusty three-round sample loop (A6) are both in A.
The one beat track in S (S7) is the sparsest, darkest and most atmospheric: trip-hop more than a rap beat.

### 7. Heavy and dark: sad and weighty beats sinister and relentless — **hunch (one pair)**
S3 (heavy guitars; clean intro, then a crushing wall with one emotional turn) and B2 (dark, arpeggiated rock riff
with a sinister twist, busy and never letting up) are both dark and heavy. What separates them is the sad,
emotional weight and the big entry in S3 against the uneasy, restless feel of B2. **[v2]** Round 1 got this wrong
by treating both simply as "dark". Now it's read as sad beats sinister (pattern 1).

### 8. House: cool, dark and shorter beats bright, flat and longer — **hunch (one pair)**
S2 (cool, dark broken-beat house, 2:35) and A2 (polished, bittersweet club house, 3:36, one hook loop for
2.5 minutes, very flat) are close cousins. The user prefers the cooler, moodier, tighter one.

### 9. Swaying triple-time pieces sit at the bottom — **hunch (two tracks)**
Both of the bottom two (B3, B4) have a waltz-like, swaying three-beat feel. So does A5. This could just reflect
their sweet mood (pattern 1).

## What doesn't seem to matter
- **Loudness and mastering.** Integrated loudness: ρ = +0.17 (p = 0.50). The loudest tracks (A6 −8.3, A4 −9.8,
  S6 −10.0 LUFS) are spread across tiers, and so are the quietest (B4 −16.6, S5 −15.8, S7 −14.6).
- **Audio quality.** Bitrate (ρ = 0.00), bandwidth (ρ = 0.03), clipping (ρ = +0.29, p = 0.24) and true peak
  (ρ = +0.23) don't track the order. S5 has a dull, 15 kHz-limited file and is still S. A4's harsh, clipped,
  low-grade copy sits mid-A, and A6's 13 kHz file is also in A. There's no sign that the user punishes poor
  sound.
- **Tempo** (ρ = −0.09) and **length**: short tracks are in S (S4 1:41, S7 1:59) and B (B3 1:34).
- **Having drums at all**: S5 has none and is S, while B4 has none and is B.
- **A hummable tune**: almost no track has one, and "melody presence" has zero correlation. The user ranks on mood
  and sound, not tunes.
- **Repetition as such**: S1, S2 and S6 all loop heavily. Repetition hurts only when the texture also stays flat
  and the mood doesn't pull you in.

## How to place a new track (checklist, v2)
1. **Mood first.** Immersive, cool, melancholy or hypnotic → leans S. Energetic but one-level, or loop-beat →
   leans A. Sweet and sunny, or tense, sinister or theatrical → leans B.
2. **Impact.** A big, satisfying arrival (drop, crash-in, hard groove) with the right mood → push toward the top.
3. **Does it breathe?** Real drops, breaks or swells move it up within its tier, or across a boundary.
4. **Hip-hop loop** → A, unless it's sparse and atmospheric (then S is possible). A loop that never changes → lower A.
5. Ignore loudness, file quality, tempo, length and genre labels.
6. **Uncertainty.** Round 1's misses were all S/A or A/B boundary calls where two patterns pulled in opposite
   directions. When that happens, widen the range toward the other side rather than splitting the difference.
